Round 1. Tell Claude who you are

8 min

Six questions, one at a time. Then a summary you check before anything else happens.

You are helping me build my first target list of companies to aim my job search at.
I am a product builder who trained and worked outside North America, and I am
looking for work in North America now. English may not be my first language, so
keep your questions and answers in short, simple sentences.

Work in three rounds. Do not skip ahead, and do not give me a list until round two.

ROUND ONE. Ask me these six questions, one at a time, and wait for my answer
before asking the next one. Do not comment on my answers, just ask the next question.

1. What is your job title now or in your most recent job, and how many years have
   you done that kind of work?
2. Name one thing you built or shipped that you are genuinely proud of. One or
   two sentences. What was it, and what happened because of it?
3. What kind of product or industry do you actually find interesting? Not what
   you think is safest. What you would read about on a weekend.
4. What size of company do you want, and why: a startup under 50 people, a mid
   size company, or a large company?
5. Which city or region are you targeting, and are you open to fully remote?
6. What is the one thing about your background you are most worried a North
   American hiring team will not understand?

After my six answers, write me a short summary of three things, and nothing else:
- MY EXPERTISE: what I am actually good at, in plain words, not job titles
- MY INTERESTS: the kind of work and product I want to be near
- MY CONSTRAINTS: location, company size, and anything else that is fixed

Show me that summary and ask me if it is right before you go on. If I say it is
wrong, fix it and show me again.

ROUND TWO comes after I confirm the summary. Wait for me.

Rules for the whole conversation:
- Simple English. Short sentences. No jargon unless you explain it right away.
- Never invent facts about me. If you do not know something, ask.
- Do not flatter me. I need this to be useful, not encouraging.
- Immigration, visa and work permit questions are out of scope. Do not guess about
  them. If it comes up, tell me to check with a real immigration professional.
Done looks like

A three line summary that sounds like you, not like a job description. If EXPERTISE reads like your old job title, tell Claude it is wrong and make it try again. This is the part everything else is built on.

Round 2. Get the list, then cut it

9 min

Twelve companies. Then you throw most of them away, which is the actual work.

The summary is right. Go to ROUND TWO.

Give me twelve companies to aim at, based on my expertise, my interests and my
constraints. Real companies that exist right now, in my target region or hiring
remotely into it.

Give me a mix on purpose:
- 4 obvious fits, where my background maps clearly onto what they do
- 4 stretch fits, where I would be reaching but the reason is real
- 4 companies I probably have not thought of, where my specific background is
  unusually useful

For each company give me exactly this, and nothing more:
- Company name and one line on what they actually do
- Which bucket it is in: obvious, stretch, or overlooked
- WHY YOU: one sentence connecting my real experience to their real problem
- One thing I should go and check for myself before I trust this

Put it in a table. Keep every line short.

Important: you may be wrong or out of date about any of these companies. Say so
plainly at the top. I am going to verify every one of them myself.
Now the real work

Cut twelve down to eight. Delete anything where the WHY YOU line does not sound true when you read it out loud. Delete anything you would be embarrassed to explain to a friend.

Claude gets company facts wrong. It does not know who is hiring today. Every company on this list is a guess until you open their real website yourself. That is your homework this week, not tonight.

Round 3. Your top three

4 min

One sentence you could actually say to a human being.

Go to ROUND THREE.

I am picking my top three from the list: [write the three names].

For each one, help me write ONE line I could actually say to a person. The shape is:

"I want to work at [company] because [something specific and true about them],
and I would be useful there because [something specific and true about me]."

Use only things I told you. Do not invent anything about me or about them.
If you do not have enough from me to fill in a blank, ask me a question instead
of guessing.

Then give me the whole thing back as a clean markdown table I can save:
company, bucket, why you, what to check, and my one line for the top three.
Save it

Paste that final table somewhere you will find it again. That file is your target list. It is the first thing you built tonight and everything else in the system points back at it.

No laptop, or the tool is not cooperating

Same 30 minutes, same result. Write these on paper.

Round 1, 8 minutes. Write your answers to the six questions above. Then write three headings and fill them in: MY EXPERTISE, MY INTERESTS, MY CONSTRAINTS.

Round 2, 9 minutes. Draw three columns: obvious, stretch, overlooked. Write as many company names as you can think of under each, then stop and cut to eight total. Beside each survivor write one line: why you.

Round 3, 4 minutes. Circle three. Write the one line for each, in this shape:

"I want to work at ______ because ______, and I would be useful there because ______."

That is the same exercise. The tool makes it faster and gives you companies you had not thought of. It does not do the deciding, and neither round 2 nor round 3 gets any easier because a machine is helping.

This week, about 20 minutes a day

Tonight was a first draft. Here is what makes it real.

  • Cut your list to five. Five real companies beats twenty maybes. A list you can hold in your head is a list you will actually use.
  • Check every one yourself. Open their real website. Are they real as described, are they hiring, do they hire at your level? Delete anything that fails.
  • Find one human at each. Not to ask them for anything. Just find out who does the work you want to do, and read what they have written or built.

That third step is the whole of Connect, the fourth part of the system, and it is the one that changes searches.
